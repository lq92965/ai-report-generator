It’s 9:17 AM on Monday. You have a blank Google Doc open, titled “Product Update: Wk 34.” Your real work hasn’t started yet. First, you have to pay the reporting tax.

You open four browser tabs: Jira for closed tickets, Amplitude for user engagement, Looker for business metrics, and a spreadsheet of user interview notes. You start the tedious ritual of copy-pasting screenshots, pulling links, and wrestling with table formatting. Two hours later, you have a six-page document detailing every minor bug fix and metric fluctuation. You post it in Slack, get three emoji reactions, and wonder if anyone read past the first paragraph.

You just spent your most valuable morning hours on a task that felt more like accounting than product management. Your report is a detailed log of the past, not a strategic look at the future.

### Your Report Is a Data Dump, Not a Decision Tool

The weekly reporting death spiral happens for a few predictable reasons. We think more detail equals more transparency. We try to anticipate every possible stakeholder question, creating a defensive document packed with preemptive appendices. The report becomes a repository for everything that happened, just in case someone asks.

This approach doesn't just waste your time; it wastes your stakeholders' time. They don’t need a changelog. They need to know if you’re winning, losing, or stuck. They need context, not a mountain of data points.

The fix is to radically shrink the scope and time you spend on reporting. Stop writing a historical archive. Start writing a tight, forward-looking briefing that takes you 15 minutes, tops.

### Automate the 'What,' So You Can Focus on the 'So What?'

Your first step is to kill the copy-paste. Manually grabbing screenshots of dashboards is a job for a robot, not a product manager.

Set up a single, permanent dashboard with your project’s key performance indicators. Use Looker, Tableau, Amplitude, or whatever tool your company uses. This dashboard is the single source of truth for the *what*. Link to it at the top of every single update you send.
