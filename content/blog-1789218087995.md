It’s 9:15 AM on Monday. You’re feeling good. You spent all last week wrestling with a complex feature, and on Friday evening, you finally birthed the PRD. You polished it, added the designs, and dropped the link into the team’s Slack channel with a cheerful: “FYI - PRD for the new checkout flow is ready for review! Let me know what you think.”

Then the pings start.

First, it’s the lead engineer in a DM: “Hey, what do we do about international taxes? Not seeing it in here.” Then a designer grabs you for a “quick huddle” to ask about an edge case you thought was obvious. By lunch, you have three separate comment threads going in the doc, a Slack thread debating the primary metric, and a calendar invite from QA to “sync on scope.”

Your entire week, which was supposed to be for Q3 planning, is now a series of ad-hoc meetings to explain the document you thought was self-explanatory. The doc drop feels efficient. It’s a lie. It’s a broadcast, not a handoff, and it creates a tax of a hundred tiny interruptions.

## The Handoff Is a Ceremony, Not a File Transfer

Shipping a PRD isn’t like sending a package. You can’t just leave it on the doorstep and walk away. A document is a starting point for a conversation, and you need to frame that conversation deliberately. Otherwise, you’re offloading the work of interpretation to your team, and every gap in the doc becomes a new meeting on your calendar.

Treating the handoff as a formal ceremony saves you from a week of chaos. It replaces dozens of scattered questions with one or two focused discussions.

### Step 1: The Pressure Test (Before You Share)

Before that link ever touches a public Slack channel, put it through a private stress test. Your goal is to find the holes before your team does.
