The chart looked great. Clean lines, a confident upward trend, and a big, bold number: “32% increase in engagement.” You pasted it from the AI analytics tool directly into your slide deck. Ten minutes into the product review, the VP of engineering squints at the screen.

“Thirty-two percent. That’s great. What’s the denominator here? Are we looking at all users or just new ones?”

You freeze. The AI just gave you the number. It didn’t show its work.

The silence hangs in the air for a few seconds too long. Your credibility just took a hit, and your beautiful chart is now a liability. The AI gave you an answer, but you didn't have the insight.

## The Black Box Is a Blind Spot

AI-powered data tools are incredible. They can sift through millions of user events, support tickets, and sales calls to find patterns in seconds. They give us answers faster than ever. But they do it inside a black box.

This speed comes at a cost: abstraction. The tool makes thousands of tiny decisions on your behalf. It decides how to handle null values. It chooses a specific statistical model. It determines what data is relevant and what is noise.

When you present a number from one of these tools without understanding those hidden decisions, you aren't presenting an analysis. You're presenting a rumor. You can’t defend it. You can’t build on it. And when it’s challenged, you’ll fold.

Your job isn’t just to find the “what.” It’s to understand the “why” and the “how.”

## Three Questions to Ask Your AI Before You Trust Its Answer

Before you copy that chart or quote that statistic, you need to cross-examine your AI. Treat it like a very fast, very eager, but very naive junior analyst. Your job is to provide the senior-level scrutiny.

### 1. “What data did you exclude?”

Omissions are more revealing than the data itself. The most dangerous assumption an AI makes is what data to ignore.

Imagine you ask it: “Summarize user sentiment for the new onboarding flow.” It comes back with “85% positive.”
