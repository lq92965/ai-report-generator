An engineer Slacks you a "quick question" about an edge case. You know the answer. You think you wrote it down. You open the Confluence spec. It’s not there. You check the JIRA ticket description. Not there either. Then you remember—you and the designer settled it in a thread two weeks ago. You spend five minutes searching Slack, find the message, copy the link, and paste it back to the engineer.

You just acted as a human API call between three different systems. You are the single source of truth. This isn't a point of pride. It's a massive scaling risk, and it’s burning out your day.

When the project context lives only in your head or is scattered across a dozen ephemeral conversations, you haven't created a system for work. You've made yourself the system.

### The Three Warring Factions of Product Information

Most teams don't have one source of truth. They have three, and they're constantly drifting out of sync.

1.  **The Archive (Confluence, Notion, Google Docs):** This is the PRD, the spec, the strategy doc. It holds the pristine, optimistic vision. It’s where you document the "why." The problem is, it’s often written once at the beginning of a project and then slowly decays, becoming a beautiful, well-formatted fossil.
2.  **The Factory Floor (JIRA, Linear, Asana):** This is the ticket. It’s what the engineers are actually building. It's the most accurate view of the current state of implementation, but it’s stripped of strategic context. The "why" is often reduced to a one-line user story.
