It’s 4 PM on a Tuesday. You need to write a one-pager outlining the new checkout analytics project. Simple enough. You have all the context in your head: the problem, the proposed solution, the key metrics. Instead of opening a doc, you open ChatGPT.

“Act as a senior product manager,” you begin. You feed it the problem statement. The output is generic. You add more context about the user journey. Better, but it sounds like a textbook. You tell it the exact metrics to track. It hallucinates a data source you don’t have. You ask it to rewrite the draft in a more direct tone. Then a more persuasive one.

An hour has passed. You now have a Frankenstein’s monster of a document. It’s grammatically correct, but it has no soul, no conviction. And you still have to spend 20 minutes editing it to sound like you. The clock ticks past 5 PM. The simple task ate your afternoon.

We were promised an AI assistant, not a time-consuming intern we have to micromanage. The trap is believing that AI is always the fastest path from A to B. Often, for a product manager with deep context, it’s a scenic detour.

### The Hidden Cost of Context Transfer

The core problem is context transfer. The knowledge is already in your brain, organized and ready. The fastest way to get it out is through your own fingers.

When you use an AI, you add a translation layer. You have to:
1.  **Translate your thoughts** into a detailed prompt.
2.  **Wait for the AI** to process and generate a response.
3.  **Review the output** for accuracy, tone, and hallucinations.
4.  **Translate your corrections** into a new, more refined prompt.
5.  **Repeat** until you get something usable.

This loop feels productive because you’re *doing something*. But it’s not always efficient. For high-context, low-creativity tasks, you’re just wasting time teaching a machine what you already know.

### When to Just Write It Yourself

Skip the AI and just open a blank document when the task fits these criteria:

*   **You have the answer.** If you’re writing a status update, a meeting summary, or a decision log, the information is already structured in your head. Dumping it onto a page is a direct path. Teaching an AI the nuances of your last sprint review is not.
*   **Your personal voice matters.** Are you trying to persuade an executive? Align a skeptical engineering lead? Build trust with a new designer? Your specific choice of words, your tone, and your demonstrated understanding of the situation are your most valuable tools. AI-generated text is sanitized and generic; it lacks the conviction of a real person with skin in the game.
*   **The task is small.** Writing a two-paragraph Slack announcement or a five-bullet-point agenda takes less time to write than it does to craft the perfect prompt to generate it. Don't spend ten minutes automating a two-minute task.

### Where AI Is a True Accelerator

This isn't an argument to abandon AI. It's an argument to use it like a scalpel, not a sledgehammer. AI is your partner for tasks where you *lack* the context or need a new perspective.

*   **Breaking the blank page.** Staring at a blinking cursor trying to start a new strategy doc? Ask an AI for five different outline structures. You won't use any of them verbatim, but one will spark an idea and get you moving. The goal is a catalyst, not a final draft.
*   **Summarizing external knowledge.** You need to get up to speed on a competitor's new feature. Feed their launch blog post and a few user reviews into the AI and ask for a summary of the value proposition and top complaints. The AI is processing raw, public data, not trying to read your mind.
*   **Reformatting and reframing.** This is AI’s superpower. "Take these messy meeting notes and turn them into a clean summary with action items." "Rewrite this technical explanation for a sales audience." "Turn this paragraph into a bulleted list." You provide the substance; the AI handles the structure.

### The 5-Minute Rule

Before you default to AI, try this. Set a timer for five minutes. Open a blank document and start writing. Just get the ideas out. No editing, no formatting.

If you have a solid foundation after five minutes, close the AI tab and finish the job yourself. You’ll be done in another ten.

If you’re still stuck, then it’s time for a thought partner. Go to the AI. Ask it for an outline. Ask it for a different angle. Use it to break your mental logjam, not to do the entire job. Your time is your most valuable asset. Stop spending it on bad prompts and start spending it on clear, direct communication.
