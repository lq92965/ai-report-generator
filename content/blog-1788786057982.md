The big meeting starts. You feel confident. You spent the last three days doing the "PM shuffle," a series of 1:1s to pre-align everyone on your new feature proposal. You talked to the engineering lead, who nodded at the technical approach. You synched with the marketing lead, who seemed happy with the positioning. You even grabbed 15 minutes with the head of sales, who gave you a thumbs-up on the customer value.

You’re the hub, and all the spokes are aligned.

Then you start presenting. Five minutes in, the engineering lead raises her hand. "Wait, I thought we were de-prioritizing the API work to make this happen? Sales said that was a dealbreaker for their biggest account."

The head of sales looks confused. "You told me the API was part of this. That was the whole point."

Suddenly, the air gets thick. Your carefully constructed alignment shatters. You didn't build a consensus. You built a series of separate, fragile, bilateral agreements. And they just collided in public.

## The Hub-and-Spoke Alignment Trap

Your pre-meetings felt productive, but they created an illusion of agreement. In each 1:1, you unconsciously tailored the pitch. You emphasized the technical elegance to the engineer and the revenue potential to the sales lead. Each person agreed to a slightly different version of the plan—the version they heard in the vacuum of a private conversation.

They never heard the trade-offs. They never saw the compromises.

The problem isn’t the 1:1 itself. The problem is using it as your primary alignment tool. When you are the only person who has heard everyone's perspective, you become a single point of failure. You become the sole keeper of context, and your translation is never perfect. The "alignment" exists only in your head.

## Stop Aligning. Start Exposing.

The goal isn't to get individual head nods in private. The goal is to get a shared commitment in public. To do that, you need to move the debate from a series of hidden conversations into a single, shared space.

Here’s how to do it.

**1. Write the Proposal Down First.**
Before a single pre-meeting, write a concise memo. Not a 10-page spec, but a 1-2 page document that clearly states:
*   **The Problem:** What user or business problem are we solving? Frame it with data.
*   **The Proposed Solution:** What are we building? Keep it high-level.
*   **The Known Trade-offs:** This is the most crucial part. What are we explicitly *not* doing? Which metric are we deprioritizing? Which customer segment will have to wait? Be painfully honest.

This document is now the single source of truth. It's the stable object around which the conversation will orbit.

**2. Circulate the Memo for Asynchronous Comments.**
Send this document to the same group you would have met with 1:1. But instead of saying "Let's chat," say, "Please read and add your comments by EOD tomorrow."

This changes everything.
