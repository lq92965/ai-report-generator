It’s 3 PM and you have a strategy review tomorrow. Your director Slacks you: “Hey, can you pull a quick one-pager on what Competitor X is up to? Just their main value props, what users are saying, and where they’re weak.”

An hour later, you’re 17 tabs deep. You have their pricing page, three outdated tech articles, a G2 profile, and a wall of text in a Google Doc. You’ve been a human copy-paste machine, and you still don’t have a clear, strategic takeaway. You’re drowning in details, not insights.

This manual grind is a time sink. It’s inconsistent and rarely surfaces the most important thing: the gap between how a company presents itself and what its customers actually experience.

Stop manually collating data. Use a large language model as a dedicated research assistant. This isn't about a single magic prompt. It's a structured, three-step process that turns raw data into a strategic summary in minutes.

### The 3-Prompt Framework for Rapid Analysis

Forget asking your AI, “Analyze my competitor.” That’s lazy and gets you a generic, hallucinated summary. You need to provide the raw material and direct the synthesis.

#### Step 1: The Marketing Deconstruction

First, let’s understand how the competitor sees themselves. Open their homepage and their pricing page. Select all, copy the text, and feed it directly to the AI.

**Prompt 1:**
`Summarize the key value propositions, target audience, and pricing model from the following text. Present it as a bulleted list. [Paste all text from their homepage and pricing page here]`

This prompt does one simple thing: it forces the AI to extract the official marketing position from the source material you provided. You get a clean summary of their intended message, free from any interpretation. This is your baseline.

#### Step 2: The User Voice Synthesis

A company’s marketing is what they want you to believe. User reviews are what people actually think.

Go to G2, Capterra, or the relevant app store. Find your competitor. Copy and paste 10-15 of the most detailed positive reviews and 10-15 of the most detailed negative reviews into your AI chat.

**Prompt 2:**
`Based on the following user reviews, identify the top 3 things users love and the top 3 recurring complaints. For each point, include a representative user quote. [Paste all the reviews here]`

This is where the magic happens. You’re not just getting a star rating; you’re getting thematic clusters of praise and frustration. You might find they market their product as “easy to use,” but the number one complaint is a “clunky UI.” This is the beginning of an insight.

#### Step 3: The Strategic Synthesis

Now you have two distinct pieces of information: the company’s story and the customer’s reality. The final step is to ask the AI to find the conflict between them.

**Prompt 3:**
`You are a senior product manager. Using the marketing summary (Context A) and the user review analysis (Context B), write a one-paragraph summary of this competitor's strategic position. Highlight the biggest disconnect between how they market their product and what users actually value or complain about.`

This prompt forces a higher level of thinking. It’s not just summarizing; it’s analyzing the gap. The output is your golden nugget—a concise, evidence-backed paragraph you can drop right into a strategy doc or a stakeholder update.

For example: “Competitor X positions itself as an all-in-one enterprise solution, but users consistently praise its simple, single-player features while complaining about buggy team collaboration. This suggests their true product-market fit is with smaller teams, despite their enterprise marketing push.”

This entire process takes less time than it took you to find their pricing page. It’s repeatable, structured, and moves you from data collection to strategic thinking almost instantly. Stop wasting your time in the tab-switching weeds. Give your AI the raw materials and the right instructions, and spend your time deciding what to do with the insight.
