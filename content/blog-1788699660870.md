A new engineer just joined your team. On day three, she DMs you a simple question: “Hey, can you point me to the doc on why we chose this specific payment processor?”

You freeze.

The answer isn't in one place. You remember the fiery Slack debate from last quarter. You recall the Google Doc where you and the tech lead listed pros and cons. You can almost picture the final email you sent to the exec team announcing the decision. The answer is everywhere and nowhere, scattered across three different tools and your own memory.

You spend the next 20 minutes digging up links, trying to reconstruct a coherent story. You just became a human search engine.

This is information fragmentation. In the moment, a quick Slack message feels efficient. A shared doc feels collaborative. An email feels official. But together, they create a crippling, invisible tax on your team’s productivity. Every decision made this way plants a seed of future confusion. Onboarding new hires is an archeological dig. Misalignment between teams isn't an exception; it's the default state.

Your job is to build a product, not to be the team's librarian. Here’s how to fix the fragmentation.
