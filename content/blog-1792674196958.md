The cursor blinked. It was 9 PM on a Tuesday, and the Q3 roadmap draft was due tomorrow. You had the company goals, a mountain of user feedback, and a vague sense of direction. You opened a new tab, navigated to your favorite AI chatbot, and typed: "Generate a product roadmap for a B2B SaaS company focused on increasing user engagement."

Seconds later, it appeared: a clean, bulleted list.

*   Implement a gamification system with badges and leaderboards.
*   Launch a user-generated content forum.
*   Develop a personalized in-app notification engine.
*   Integrate with five new third-party tools.

It looked professional. It sounded plausible. But it wasn't a roadmap. It was a wish list. A generic collection of tactics, completely disconnected from your customers, your team's capacity, and your company's unique position in the market.

You didn't get a strategy. You got a menu.

### The AI Gave You Tactics, Not a Vision

An AI chatbot is a powerful pattern-matching machine. It has ingested a vast amount of text from the internet, including countless blog posts about product management. When you ask for a roadmap, it serves you the most common, statistically likely features associated with "user engagement."

It can’t know:
*   Your company’s strategic imperative to expand into the enterprise market.
*   The engineering team’s ongoing struggle with tech debt from the last "engagement" push.
*   The VP of Sales’s constant feedback that the onboarding flow is losing you six-figure deals.
*   The critical insight buried in a customer interview last week about a workflow gap no one else has solved.

The AI-generated list is strategically empty. Gamification might sound good, but does it solve a real problem for your specific power users, or is it just a distraction? A new forum requires a community manager and a moderation plan. Do you have those resources? The AI doesn't ask. It just suggests.

You outsourced the thinking, not just the typing. The core of your job—synthesizing disparate information into a coherent, opinionated plan—was handed to a tool that has no opinions.

### Use the AI as a Sparring Partner, Not a Strategist

The mistake wasn't using the tool. It was asking the wrong question. Don't ask an AI for a finished strategy. Use it to accelerate your own strategic thinking. Treat it like a brilliant, but inexperienced, junior PM who needs clear direction.

Here’s how to do it right.

#### 1. Provide the Strategic Context First

Your first prompt should be a data dump of your own knowledge. You set the guardrails. The AI fills in the blanks.

**Bad Prompt:** `"Give me a roadmap for next quarter."`

**Good Prompt:**
`"I am the PM for a project management tool. Our company's top-level goal for H2 is to reduce churn by 10% in our 'Pro' customer segment. Our user research shows these customers struggle most with project setup and team collaboration. Our primary competitors are Asana and Monday.com. Engineering has two available pods (8 engineers total). Given this context, brainstorm three potential strategic themes for our Q3 roadmap. For each theme, propose a key outcome metric."`

Now you’re not asking for *the* answer. You’re asking for structured possibilities based on the constraints *you* defined.

#### 2. Ask It to Generate Options, Not a Single Path

The AI’s first output is rarely the best one. Its strength is generating volume. Use that to explore the problem space before you commit to a solution.

Let’s say one theme from the previous prompt was "Accelerate Time-to-First-Value." Now you can dig deeper.

**Good Follow-up Prompt:**
`"For the theme 'Accelerate Time-to-First-Value,' generate five different initiative ideas. For each idea, list the potential pros and cons, and estimate the relative engineering effort on a scale of Small, Medium, Large."`

The output might include:
*   **Idea:** Pre-built project templates (Effort: Medium)
*   **Idea:** An interactive setup wizard (Effort: Large)
*   **Idea:** A sample project with dummy data (Effort: Small)

You just turned the AI into a brainstorming machine, saving you hours of whiteboarding. You still have to make the hard choices, weigh the trade-offs, and validate the ideas, but you're starting from a much richer place.

#### 3. Use It to Flesh Out the Tactics

Once you’ve used your own judgment to select a strategic direction—let's say you're building the project templates—you can hand the tactical work back to the AI.

**Good Tactical Prompts:**
*   `"Write five user stories for a new user selecting a project template."`
*   `"Draft a one-paragraph announcement for our monthly newsletter about this upcoming feature."`
*   `"List ten potential risks or edge cases we should consider when building project templates."`

This is where the tool excels. It handles the boilerplate, the first drafts, and the grunt work, freeing you up to talk to customers, align stakeholders, and manage the complexity that only a human can.

A roadmap tells a story about why the future will be better for your customers. An AI can help you write the sentences and organize the paragraphs. But you, the product manager, must still be the author of the story.
