The standup channel dies around week three. Day one, everyone posts. Day five, two people post and three react with 👍. Week three, it's a ghost town and you're back to booking a 15-minute call that runs 25.

I've watched this happen on four teams. The fix isn't a better bot or a stricter rule. It's changing what the standup is *for*. Most async standups fail because they ask people to narrate their day. Nobody wants to do that in writing, and nobody wants to read it. If you reframe the async standup as a **blocker-and-decision feed** — not a diary — it survives.

Here's the format I've landed on, and the mechanics that keep it alive past the novelty phase.

## Why the standard three-question standup rots

"Yesterday / Today / Blockers" is a verbal format bolted onto text. In a meeting, the value is in the side-channel: someone hears a blocker and jumps in. In text, that side-channel disappears, so the "blockers" line becomes a place to write "none" and move on. The format rewards people for having nothing to say.

The other failure mode: it becomes a status report nobody asked for. If your standup reads like a mini status report, you've just duplicated a document you already have. (If you're not sure whether your team's updates are status or progress, [the difference matters more than it sounds](#) — but that's a separate post.)

An async standup should answer one question: **what does the team need to know or decide before tomorrow?** Everything else is optional.

## The format: three lines, one of them mandatory

Post this in the channel as the pinned template:

```
BLOCKED: <what's stuck, who can unblock, by when>
DECIDED: <any call you made that affects others>
FYI: <optional, one line, skip if nothing>
```

That's it. Three fields, and only BLOCKED is truly mandatory — write "none" if you're clear.

Why this works:

- **BLOCKED is the only line that demands action.** It routes to a person, not a status. If someone is blocked and you can help, you reply in-thread. If nobody's blocked, the standup takes 20 seconds to read.
- **DECIDED captures the thing that usually gets lost.** Someone picks a library, changes a copy string, or agrees to a scope cut in a side DM. Writing it here means the next person doesn't re-litigate it. This is a lightweight cousin of a full decision log — same instinct, less ceremony.
- **FYI is a pressure valve.** People who want to share more can, but it's clearly optional, so the format doesn't punish brevity.

## The mechanics that keep it from rotting

Format alone won't save you. Three operational habits do most of the work.

**1. Post by a fixed time, but don't police it.** Pick 10am local. If someone misses, don't nag in the channel — that turns the standup into a compliance exercise. A quiet DM the first two times, then let it go. The people who need to post will post because the format is cheap.

**2. Reply in-thread, never in the channel.** The channel should read as a clean feed of three-line posts. Discussion happens in threads under the BLOCKED line. This keeps the feed scannable for anyone catching up, which is the whole point of async.

**3. Kill it on Fridays.** A Friday async standup is where the rot starts, because nobody has a tomorrow to be blocked on. Skip it. If you need a Friday touchpoint, that's a different artifact — a short end-of-week note, not a standup.

## What to do with the BLOCKED lines

This is where most teams leave value on the table. At the end of each day (or start of the next), the PM or tech lead skims the feed and does one thing: **route the blockers.** A blocked line with no reply by end of day is a signal — either the person needs help finding the right unblocker, or the block is bigger than one thread.

Keep a running list of open blockers somewhere outside the channel. A simple doc or a pinned message with resolved items struck through works. Don't build a dashboard for this. The moment you build a standup dashboard, you've recreated the meeting in a worse medium.

## When to escalate out of the standup

Some blockers don't belong in a thread. If a BLOCKED line has been open for two days with no movement, that's not a standup problem anymore — it's a risk. Pull it into your weekly status or health check and give it a proper owner and date. The async standup is a *triage* tool, not a tracking system. Treat it that way and it stays fast.

A quick rule I use: if a blocker needs more than three thread replies to resolve, it's a meeting. Book 10 minutes, resolve it, post the DECIDED line back into the standup so the record is complete.

## A filled-in example

Here's what a real day looks like in this format, from a small platform team:

```
BLOCKED: Auth token refresh needs a decision on TTL — @maya, can we do 15m or 30m? Blocking the mobile handoff. Need by Wed.
DECIDED: Going with the existing retry library instead of adding a new one. Saves a week.
FYI: Staging is flaky again, infra ticket #4412.
```

Two people reply in-thread. Maya answers by lunch. The DECIDED line saves someone from re-opening the library question next week. The FYI is a heads-up, not an ask. Total reading time: 15 seconds.

Compare that to a "yesterday/today/blockers" post that says "worked on auth, continuing auth, no blockers" — which tells you nothing and trains everyone to skim.

## The one rule that matters

If you take nothing else: **an async standup should be skimmable in under a minute and actionable in under five.** If it's neither, it's a status report wearing a standup costume, and it will rot like every other one.

Try the three-line format for two weeks. If the channel is still alive on day 14, you've found something worth keeping. If it isn't, you've learned that your team doesn't need a daily touchpoint — and that's a useful finding too.
