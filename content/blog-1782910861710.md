It’s 10:17 AM. You just left the daily stand-up where you promised to clarify a user story. A Slack notification pops up from marketing—they need final copy approval for the new landing page. As you open the Google Doc, an email arrives from your director with the subject line "Quick question on Q3 numbers." You pivot to find the dashboard link. While you’re pulling that up, the engineer from stand-up pings you again: "Hey, any update on that story?"

You feel incredibly busy. You feel essential. But you’re not making progress. You're thrashing.

In computing, thrashing is when a system spends more time switching between tasks than actually doing productive work. Its resources are consumed by the overhead of context switching. Sound familiar? This isn't effective multitasking. It's a state of perpetual, low-impact reactivity. And it’s the default state for many product managers.

The cost isn’t just your sanity. The cost is a shallow roadmap, a poorly defined feature, and a strategy that never gets the deep thought it requires.

### The Real Price of a "Quick Switch"

We dismiss interruptions as the cost of doing business. A "quick question" on Slack feels harmless. But research shows it takes an average of 23 minutes to fully regain focus after being interrupted.

When you bounce from a technical spec to a marketing campaign to a financial forecast, you’re paying that tax three times in ten minutes. You never get deep enough into any single topic to do your best work.

This leads to specific failures:
*   **Shallow PRDs:** Your documents become a collection of stakeholder requests rather than a coherent vision because you never had two uninterrupted hours to think through the customer problem.
*   **Reactive Decisions:** You approve the copy because it’s urgent, not because it’s right. You agree to a timeline because you’re on the spot, not because you’ve vetted the dependencies.
