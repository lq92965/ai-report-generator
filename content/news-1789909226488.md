A support engineer types a query into the company’s new internal chatbot. "What's the rollback procedure for a failed deployment of microservice `ares-v3`?" The answer comes back instantly, formatted in perfect Markdown, citing three separate documents from the internal wiki. It looks authoritative. It feels correct.

It is also completely wrong. Following the procedure would wipe the production database.

The problem isn't that the model hallucinated. The problem is that it did its job perfectly. The Large Language Model, grounded by a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) system, faithfully cited its sources. The sources were the problem. One was a hastily written summary of a post-mortem, generated by a different AI. Another was a draft plan from two years ago, written by an engineer who has since left the company. The third was a copy-paste from a public forum, subtly altered by a well-meaning but confused project manager.

RAG was supposed to be the leash that kept these models tethered to reality. By forcing the AI to pull answers from a curated set of documents—our documents, our data, our supposed truth—we thought we had solved the fabrication problem. We built a library and told the AI it could only read the books we put on the shelves.

We forgot to hire a librarian.

The feedback loop is now closing. Marketing teams use ChatGPT to generate blog posts, which are then added to the official company website. That website becomes a source for the next-generation RAG system. Junior developers, pressed for time, use an AI to "document" their code, checking the plausible-sounding output directly into the repository. The AI-written documentation is then indexed and becomes the ground truth for the chatbot that's supposed to help the next developer.

We are feeding the machine its own exhaust.

This isn't just about a few flawed documents. This is a systemic pollution of the very data we're counting on to keep AI factual. The institutional memory, once a collection of human experience and hard-won lessons, is being overwritten with a high-gloss, grammatically perfect layer of synthetic text. Every AI-generated meeting summary that misses a key nuance, every AI-written quarterly report that papers over a difficult truth, becomes a permanent, citable fact in the digital record.

The stakes are not academic. When a system built to provide financial advice starts referencing AI-generated market analyses, it creates a closed loop of logic detached from the actual market. When a medical diagnostic tool is grounded in a corpus of medical literature that is itself increasingly populated by AI-summarized—and subtly simplified—research papers, the potential for error multiplies silently.

We celebrated the automation of writing without building the tools for verification. The promise was that we could accelerate knowledge creation. The reality is that we are accelerating knowledge degradation. The source of truth is no longer a stable foundation. It's a room of mirrors, and the model we built to show us the way is now just showing us reflections of itself. The hallucination isn't coming from the black box anymore. It’s coming from the library.
