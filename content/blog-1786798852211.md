The demo is going perfectly. Your engineer is walking through the new feature, the stakeholders are nodding, and you’re mentally drafting the launch announcement. Then it happens. The VP of Sales, who just joined the call, unmutes.

“This looks great,” she says. “Just a simple question: can we make that button export a custom PDF for the client’s quarterly business review? Our enterprise deals are asking for that.”

The air goes out of the room. Your engineer’s face freezes. You know, and your team knows, that this is not a “simple question.” It’s a completely new feature, a Pandora’s Box of edge cases, dependencies, and at least two sprints of work.

But in that moment, all eyes are on you. Your response will determine whether your team spends the next week building what you planned, or chasing a ghost dreamed up in the middle of a demo.

## Why the 'Simple Question' is a Landmine

An executive’s drive-by question feels harmless. It’s often born from a genuine customer insight. But treating it as a simple question is a critical error.

It’s a context-shattering missile aimed at your team’s focus. It ignores the six weeks of research, design, and debate that led to the current plan. It puts your engineer on the spot to make an impossible-to-keep commitment.

Worse, it creates a shadow backlog. If you say “we’ll look into it,” you’ve just created a new P0 ticket in the VP’s mind. You’ve implicitly prioritized their idea over everything else your team is supposed to be doing. Fail to deliver, and you’ve broken a promise you never should have made.

## Your Instincts Are Probably Wrong

Most PMs fall into one of three traps when this happens.

*   **The People-Pleaser:** “Great idea! We’ll absolutely look into that.” You’ve bought yourself 30 seconds of harmony and a month of pain. Your team’s morale plummets as they watch their well-laid plans get bulldozed.
*   **The Gatekeeper:** “That’s not in scope for this quarter.” You’re right, but you sound defensive and dismissive. You’ve shut down the conversation, but you’ve also signaled that you aren’t a strategic partner. You’ve damaged a critical relationship.
