It’s 9:17 AM on Monday. You have three browser tabs open: a JIRA board showing last week’s completed stories, a Looker dashboard with the latest adoption numbers, and a Google Doc from last week’s status report. Your mission, which you’ve reluctantly accepted, is to copy and paste pieces from the first two tabs into a fresh version of the third.

You wrangle the data, add some bullet points, and hit "send" on the email to your stakeholders. For a brief moment, you feel productive. But you know the truth. The report you just sent is a snapshot of a world that no longer exists. A critical bug was found at 9:18 AM. The designer just dropped a revised Figma link in Slack. Your report is a photograph of a moving train, and it’s already obsolete.

This is the problem with treating your status update as an event. It’s a time-consuming, one-off broadcast that’s stale on arrival. You’re not creating a source of truth; you’re creating a weekly information relic.

### From Broadcast to Home Base

The alternative is to stop thinking of your update as an email you send and start thinking of it as a destination you maintain.
