It’s Tuesday morning. Your CEO is scrolling through the company’s project dashboard. Your project, “Phoenix,” is a neat, reassuring green circle. All good. They move on to the next line item.

They don’t see the Slack channel where your lead engineer just flagged that the new authentication service is built on a deprecated library. They don’t know your designer is two weeks behind because the user research invalidated three core assumptions. And they certainly don’t know you spent all of yesterday in a tense meeting trying to negotiate a dependency with a team that has zero incentive to help you.

Your status is green. But your project is a dumpster fire.

You’ve just delivered a watermelon report: green on the outside, red on the inside. This isn’t protecting your team. It’s hiding a bomb. When it explodes, the blast radius won’t just take out the timeline; it will take out your credibility.
