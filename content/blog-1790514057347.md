You’re scrolling through a Slack channel, your eyes glazing over. Three weeks ago, your team made a critical call on the new checkout flow. Now, the VP of Sales is asking why you didn’t choose the one-click option. You remember the meeting. You know there was a good reason—something about API latency and fraud risk.

You open the Google Doc titled “Checkout Sync - Notes.” It’s a 2,000-word wall of text. It's a faithful, minute-by-minute account of who said what. It's a perfect transcript. And it's completely useless.

The signal—the decision, the rationale, the trade-offs—is buried in the noise of the conversation. You can’t find the answer, so you can’t defend the decision. The conversation starts all over again.

We’ve been taught to take "good notes." But for a product manager, a transcript of a conversation is a failure. It captures activity, not outcome. It tells you what happened in the meeting, but not what happens next. It’s a record of attendance, not a record of alignment.

Stop writing meeting transcripts. Start authoring Decision Records.

### The Transcript Is for You. The Record Is for the Team.

A transcript is a private memory aid. It helps the person who wrote it remember the conversation. For everyone else, it’s an archaeological dig. They have to sift through quotes, tangents, and half-formed thoughts to guess at the conclusion.

A Decision Record is a public artifact. It’s a concise, clear statement of a conclusion and its context. It’s designed for the person who *wasn’t* in the room. It’s built to be referenced, shared, and defended. It short-circuits debate and prevents relitigation.

Switching from one to the other isn't about writing more. It's about synthesizing better.

### Anatomy of a Lean Decision Record

Your Decision Record doesn't need to be a ten-page document. It should be brutally efficient. After your next decision-making meeting, skip the transcript and write this instead.

**1. Context (1 Sentence)**
What problem were we trying to solve or what question were we trying to answer? Link to the relevant PRD, ticket, or strategy doc.

*Example: "We needed to select a final design direction for the Q3 onboarding redesign to unblock the engineering team."*

**2. The Decision (1-2 Sentences)**
State the decision in the most direct, unambiguous way possible. Use an active voice. Leave no room for interpretation.

*Example: "We are moving forward with Design Variant B (link to Figma). We will not pursue Variant A at this time."*

**3. Rationale & Evidence (2-3 Bullets)**
This is the "why." What data, user research, technical constraints, or strategic principles led to this choice? Be specific.

*Example:*
*   *In user testing, Variant B had a 30% higher task completion rate.*
*   *Variant B reuses existing front-end components, reducing engineering effort by an estimated 3 sprints.*
*   *The primary call-to-action aligns with our company-wide goal of increasing self-serve adoption.*

**4. Alternatives Considered (1-2 Bullets)**
Briefly name the other options and, crucially, state why they were rejected. This is your best defense against future "what-about-ism." It shows you did your due diligence.

*Example:*
*   *We considered Variant A, but its reliance on a new third-party API introduced unacceptable latency and risk.*
*   *We discussed a hybrid approach, but it would have created a confusing user experience without clear benefits.*

**5. Next Steps & Owners (Bulleted List)**
Who is doing what, and by when? This turns the decision into action.

*Example:*
*   *PM (Sarah): Update the JIRA epic with the final designs by EOD Tuesday.*
*   *Design (Leo): Provide final redlines and assets to the engineering team by Wednesday.*
*   *Eng (Mike): Break the epic down into implementation tickets for sprint planning on Thursday.*

### How to Make it a Habit

This isn't just a template; it's a change in your meeting workflow.

**End the meeting with the record.** Don't just say "Great meeting, thanks everyone." Stop five minutes before the scheduled end time and synthesize the decision record live. Go through the five points out loud. "Okay, to confirm: The decision is B. The reason is X and Y. We're not doing A because of Z. Sarah, you have the next step. Does anyone see this differently?"

**Post it immediately in a predictable place.** A decision that nobody can find is the same as a decision that was never made. Create a single, central home for these records—a dedicated Confluence page, a Notion database, even a pinned post in a team Slack channel. The medium matters less than the consistency.

Your job isn't to be the team stenographer. It's to be the team's source of clarity. A transcript proves you were listening. A Decision Record proves you created alignment.
