The launch dashboard is green. Adoption is climbing. You close your laptop Friday afternoon feeling pretty good. Then Monday hits.

A customer success manager pulls you into a tense call with an angry client who was promised the feature would solve a problem it was never designed to address. The top-voted question in the company all-hands is about why marketing’s announcement used a screenshot from the old UI. A support engineer Slacks you, “What am I supposed to tell people who hit this error state? The internal KB article is just a link to the JIRA ticket.”

Your feature isn't failing. Your internal story is. You spent six months meticulously crafting the product but only six minutes crafting the explanation for your own team.

### The Changelog is Not the Story

We get so buried in PRDs, Figma files, and sprint planning that we forget a crucial truth: the teams selling, marketing, and supporting your feature were not in those meetings. They didn't read the 147 comments on the design doc. To them, your masterpiece is just another bullet point in the release notes.

When we fail to translate the nuance, the trade-offs, and the core customer problem, we create organizational chaos. We force every internal team to invent their own narrative.

*   **Sales** invents a narrative to win a deal.
*   **Support** invents a narrative to close a ticket.
*   **Marketing** invents a narrative to get more clicks.

Soon, you have a dozen different versions of your product living in the wild. This isn't a communication problem; it's a product problem. The internal narrative is part of the feature.
