The email from the CTO landed with a triumphant thud: "To accelerate innovation, we've provisioned a ChatGPT Enterprise seat for every employee!" The Slack channels erupted in 🎉 and 🚀 emojis. We imagined a future of hyper-efficient meetings, instantly generated specs, and code that wrote itself.

A month later, the reality is less rocket ship, more bumper cars.

Our lead engineer, Mark, pastes entire codebases into the chat window to "refactor," blissfully unaware of our data privacy policy. Sarah, in marketing, uses it to generate blog posts, but her prompts are so generic the output reads like a LinkedIn bot having a fever dream. Half the product team tried it once, got a mediocre answer, and went straight back to their old Google Doc templates.

We didn’t roll out a productivity tool. We rolled out chaos. We gave everyone a powerful engine but no steering wheel, no map, and no rules of the road. Now we have 50 different workflows, zero consistency, and a nagging fear that someone is about to paste our Q3 strategy into a public model.

The problem isn't the tool. It's the absence of a playbook. Here’s how to fix it.

### Stop Boiling the Ocean. Standardize 3-5 Use Cases.

The promise of "use AI for anything" is a trap. It leads to decision paralysis or low-effort misuse. Instead of a vague mandate, identify a handful of specific, high-value, low-risk tasks for your team.

Don't just say, "Use it for research." Get specific. Your initial list might look like this:
*   **Summarizing user interview transcripts:** Turn a 60-minute raw text file into bulleted key themes and memorable quotes.
*   **Drafting JIRA acceptance criteria:** Feed it a user story and ask for a comprehensive list of "Given/When/Then" scenarios.
*   **Generating synthetic user personas:** Provide market data and interview themes to create a detailed, realistic persona.
*   **Brainstorming API endpoint names:** Give it a resource and desired actions, and ask for clear, RESTful naming conventions.

By defining the "what," you take the cognitive load off the team and focus their energy on a few tasks where the AI can provide immediate, tangible value.
