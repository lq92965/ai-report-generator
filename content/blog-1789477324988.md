It felt so efficient. Your lead engineer pinged you on Slack with a question about an edge case. You hashed it out in a few messages, made a call, and sent a thumbs-up emoji. Decision made. You moved on to the next fire.

Then Monday happened.

The marketing lead asks how the new behavior will be messaged. You forward a screenshot of the DM. Later, a support agent asks why a customer is seeing something unexpected. You type out the rationale again. The next day, a different engineer working on a related component is confused by the change. You find yourself re-explaining the context, the trade-offs, and the final call for the third time.

That "quick" decision in a direct message wasn't efficient. It was a loan taken out against future alignment, and the interest is killing your calendar. You traded a single, clear explanation for a dozen fragmented, time-consuming ones.

The problem isn’t the tool. It’s a channel mismatch. We reach for tools of convenience for conversations that demand permanence. We optimize for the speed of the initial answer, not the long-term cost of discoverability.

## Your Communication Channel Map: Right Message, Right Place

Think of your communication tools not as a messy drawer but as a purpose-built workshop. Each tool has a specific job. Using the wrong one is like trying to drive a nail with a screwdriver. It’s messy, ineffective, and frustrating.

Here’s a simple map to fix it.

### Use DMs and Ephemeral Chat for: Coordination

This is for real-time logistics. The conversational glue that holds the day together.

*   **Examples:** "Hey, can you look at this ticket when you have a sec?" "Running 5 minutes late for standup." "Here’s the Figma link you asked for."
*   **Litmus Test:** If this message vanished forever after 24 hours, would the project suffer? If the answer is no, it belongs here. It’s temporary and tactical.
