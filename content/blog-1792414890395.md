It’s 10 AM on Tuesday. You just launched a redesigned checkout flow. Now you’re staring at the support queue. The ticket count ticks up: 47, 58, 71. Your support lead Slacks you: “Seeing a lot of complaints about promo codes. Is something broken?”

You dive in. You read one ticket from a user who couldn’t apply a discount. Then another about a confusing error message. A third is from someone who thought the discount applied automatically. After an hour of this, you have a handful of anecdotes but no clear pattern. You’re lost in the weeds, and the problem is still growing.

Manually sifting through qualitative feedback is a trap. It’s slow, biased by the last ticket you read, and impossible to scale. You can’t see the forest for the trees.

## The Old Way: Spreadsheets and Exhaustion

For years, the standard approach was to export a CSV from Zendesk or Intercom. You’d open it in Google Sheets, maybe create a pivot table, and spend the next three hours trying to tag tickets into categories.

This process is fundamentally broken. By the time you’ve synthesized the feedback, the engineering team has already moved on to the next sprint. Your hard-won insight arrives a day late. Worse, this method is riddled with confirmation bias. You start looking for the promo code issue your support lead mentioned, and suddenly every ticket seems to be about promo codes. You miss the quieter, more critical signal hidden in the noise.

## A Better Way: Thematic Analysis with an AI Prompt

You don’t need to read every single ticket. You need to understand the major themes. This is a perfect job for a large language model. Using an AI to pre-process feedback isn't about replacing your judgment; it's about giving it a massive head start. The AI does the sorting and clustering, freeing you up to do the actual product work: validating the insights and making decisions.

It turns a multi-hour task into a ten-minute job.

## The 3-Step Process You Can Use Today

This isn't theoretical. You can do this right now with a tool like ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini. All you need is your raw data and a well-structured prompt.

### Step 1: Get Your Raw Data

Export the feedback from its source. This could be support tickets, App Store reviews, NPS survey responses, or messages from a community Slack channel. You don't need a perfectly formatted file. All you really need is a single column containing the customer’s raw, unfiltered message. A simple copy-paste into a text file or a single-column CSV is enough.

### Step 2: Craft Your Prompt

The quality of your output depends entirely on the quality of your input. Don't just ask the AI to "summarize these tickets." Be specific. Give it a role, a clear format, and constraints.

Here is a prompt you can copy and paste:

```
You are an expert product analyst specializing in customer feedback. I will provide you with a list of raw support tickets from a recent product launch.

Your task is to perform a detailed thematic analysis. Follow these instructions exactly:

1.  Identify the top 5-7 recurring themes or problem areas.
2.  For each theme, create a short, descriptive name (e.g., "Promo Code Field Not Working," "Confusion Over Shipping Costs").
3.  For each theme, pull 2-3 direct, verbatim quotes from the tickets that best exemplify the problem. Do not summarize or change the quotes.
4.  For each theme, provide an estimated percentage of the total tickets that fall into that category.
5.  Present the final output as a clean markdown table with the columns: "Theme," "Estimated %," and "Example Quotes."

Here is the raw data:

[PASTE YOUR LIST OF TICKETS OR FEEDBACK HERE]
```

This prompt works because it forces the AI to be structured. It asks for themes, evidence (quotes), and magnitude (percentages), giving you a rich, actionable summary.

### Step 3: Interpret and Act

The AI’s output is a high-quality draft, not a final decree. Your job is to apply your context and critical thinking.

Look at the themes. Do they ring true? Read the quotes. Do they genuinely support the theme the AI has identified? Sometimes an AI will misinterpret sarcasm or group unrelated issues.

This is where you connect the dots. The AI might tell you 20% of tickets are about "Confusion Over Shipping Costs." You know that shipping cost confusion is a primary driver of cart abandonment. Now you don't just have a list of complaints; you have a hypothesis tied directly to a core business metric. You can write a clear ticket, propose a design change, or update your FAQ with confidence, all backed by data you generated in minutes.

## What This Unlocks (And What It Doesn't)

This technique gives you speed. You can get a pulse check on a new feature launch while the launch meeting is still happening. It helps you find the "unknown unknowns"—the surprising issues you weren't even looking for.

But it isn't a silver bullet. The AI finds the *what*, not the *why*. It tells you that users are getting stuck, but it doesn't tell you about the frustration or the context behind their struggle. The themes it surfaces are pointers. They tell you exactly which customers you need to contact for a follow-up interview.

Your job isn’t to be a human text parser. It’s to identify the most important problem, understand its impact, and rally the team to solve it. Stop manually digging for patterns in a mountain of text. Use the machine to surface the critical themes so you can spend your time on the work that actually matters: building a better product.
