The demo went perfectly. The engineering team is taking a well-deserved victory lap in the #releases Slack channel. You just shipped a feature that solves a real customer problem. Then, your Product Marketing Manager drops a message: “This is amazing! Can you send me the key talking points, target audience, and value props for the launch announcement?”

You freeze.

You have a 12-page PRD that details every user story, technical constraint, and acceptance criterion. You have Figma links, JIRA epics, and a Loom video of the final build. You have everything you need to *build* the feature. You have nothing to help anyone *sell* it.

This is the moment you realize the PRD is an internal-facing document for your build team. Your go-to-market (GTM) partners—marketing, sales, customer success—need something else entirely. They need the bridge between what the feature *is* and why a customer should *care*. When that bridge doesn’t exist, you get frantic, last-minute copywriting, inconsistent sales pitches, and a launch that lands with a thud.

You don't need another meeting. You need a GTM Handoff Brief.

## The Single Source of Truth for Your Launch

The GTM Handoff Brief is a simple document that translates your product work into a market-ready message. It’s not a replacement for the PRD; it’s a companion to it. You should start drafting it the moment you have problem-solution fit, and you should co-create it with your PMM.

It prevents the game of telephone where your technical explanation gets watered down with each re-telling. It becomes the canonical source for every blog post, sales deck, and knowledge base article.

Here’s what goes into it.

### 1. The One-Sentence Pitch

Force yourself to be concise. If you can’t explain it in a single sentence, your GTM team won’t be able to either. Follow this simple structure:

**For [target customer] who [has this problem], our product is a [market category] that provides [key benefit].**

Example: "For finance teams who manually reconcile invoices, our new feature is an accounting integration that automatically syncs payments."

### 2. The Problem, In the Customer's Voice

Don’t describe your solution yet. Describe the pain. Pull direct quotes from user interviews or support tickets. This grounds the entire launch in a real-world struggle.

*   **Bad:** "Users lack a way to sync data between our platform and their accounting software."
*   **Good:** "I spend the first three days of every month just exporting CSVs and manually matching line items in QuickBooks. Last month, one typo cost me an entire afternoon."

### 3. Key Benefits (Not Features)

Your GTM team needs to sell outcomes, not functionality. Translate your feature list into tangible customer benefits. Aim for three.

*   **Feature:** Implemented OAuth 2.0 for integrations.
*   **Benefit:** Connect your accounting software in two clicks, without ever sharing a password.

*   **Feature:** Added a background sync worker.
*   **Benefit:** Your data is always up-to-date, automatically. No more manual refreshes.

*   **Feature:** Built a transaction reconciliation view.
*   **Benefit:** Find and fix discrepancies in minutes, not hours.

### 4. Who Is This For (and Who Is It NOT For)?

Get specific. "All users" is not a target audience. A well-defined audience helps marketing focus their spend and helps sales qualify leads. Equally important is defining who the feature *isn't* for. This prevents mis-sold expectations and future support headaches.

*   **This is for:** Teams of 10-50 people using QuickBooks Online or Xero. They process over 100 invoices a month.
*   **This is NOT for:** Enterprise customers using custom ERPs, or freelancers with very simple accounting needs.

### 5. What Makes This Different?

Your customers have alternatives. They can use a competitor, a messy workaround, or just ignore the problem. You need to arm your GTM team with clear differentiators. Why is your solution the best choice?

Maybe it’s deeper integration. Maybe it’s superior design. Maybe it’s simply being included in their current plan instead of costing extra. Be explicit.

This brief isn’t just another document to create. It’s a focusing mechanism. It forces you to think like a marketer and a salesperson long before the code is finished. Share the first draft early with your PMM and make them a true partner. By the time you’re ready to launch, they won’t be asking you what the feature does. They’ll already be telling the world.
