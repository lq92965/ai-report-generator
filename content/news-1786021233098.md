The logistics model was perfect. For six months, it optimized delivery routes for a national CPG brand, shaving millions from fuel costs. Then, last Tuesday, it started routing a third of the Dallas fleet through a school zone, right at dismissal time. No one was hurt. But for two hours, a grid of residential streets was choked with semi-trucks. The algorithm didn't know the city had rerouted traffic around new construction that started last month. The model, a multi-million dollar investment, was already out of date.

This is the quiet failure condition for artificial intelligence that no one is pricing in. We talk about spectacular hallucinations and catastrophic jailbreaks. The more common, more insidious threat is simple decay. The world changes, and the model does not. Every large language model, every predictive algorithm, every AI system deployed today is a snapshot of the past. It is a static, brilliantly detailed photograph of a world that has already moved on.
