The deploy notification hits the `#engineering` Slack channel. You see the green checkmark. It’s live. You post a quick “It’s shipped!” message in the main company channel with a link to the JIRA epic.

Silence.

Then, an hour later, a message from the sales lead: “Team is asking what the one-liner is for this new thing. Is there a deck?”

A support agent follows up: “A customer just ran into an error on the new page. Is this a known issue?”

You built the feature. You ran the sprint. You merged the code. But you forgot the last, most critical step: the handoff. A feature no one understands is a feature that doesn't exist. Your JIRA tickets and technical changelogs are meaningless to the people who talk to customers every day.

They don’t need your sprint notes. They need a translator.

### Your JIRA Link Is Not Communication

Sending a link to a JIRA ticket is like handing someone a box of IKEA parts with no instructions. All the pieces are there, but nobody knows how they fit together or what they’re supposed to build.

*   **Sales** needs to know the *pain* it solves, not the acceptance criteria.
*   **Marketing** needs a *story*, not a list of sub-tasks.
*   **Customer Support** needs to know the *limitations*, not the pull request history.

When you fail to translate your work, you force every other department to do it for you. The result is a mess of inconsistent messaging, confused customers, and frustrated teams.

### The 5-Point Internal Release Note

Stop forwarding technical artifacts. Start writing a simple, scannable update that gives each team exactly what they need. Create a template in Confluence or a Google Doc and make it your post-launch ritual.

Here’s what goes in it.

**1. The Elevator Pitch: What is it?**
Start with a single, jargon-free sentence. This is the headline. Assume they will read nothing else.

*   **Bad:** *Shipped Project Phoenix V2 (JIRA-123), which refactors the backend service for the user settings module.*
*   **Good:** *Users can now invite teammates with a single click from the project dashboard.*

**2. The Customer Problem: Why did we build it?**
Connect the feature directly to a customer pain point. This gives the work meaning and provides the "why" for the entire company. Use a real (anonymized) customer quote if you have one.

*   **Example:** *“Previously, adding a new team member required navigating to a separate admin panel, which was slow and confusing. A customer told us, ‘I just want to add my new hire to the project without having to hunt for the settings page.’”*

**3. How It Works (The 30-Second Demo)**
Show, don’t just tell. A 15-30 second GIF or a short Loom video is perfect. Record yourself performing the main user action. This is infinitely more valuable than a long paragraph of text. Below the visual, you can add a few simple bullet points outlining the steps.

**4. Talking Points for Sales & Marketing**
Give your go-to-market teams the exact language they can use. This isn't about controlling them; it's about equipping them.

*   **Target Audience:** Who is this for? (e.g., "Team admins on our Pro plan.")
*   **Key Benefits (in their words):** What can the customer do now? (e.g., "Onboard new users in seconds," "Reduce administrative overhead.")
*   **Sample Pitch:** A sentence or two they can copy and paste into an email. (e.g., "I know you mentioned team setup was a hassle, so I wanted to share our new one-click invite feature.")

**5. The "Heads-Up" for Support**
Your support team is your first line of defense. Arm them with the information they need *before* a customer finds a problem. This builds trust and reduces escalations.

*   **Known Limitations:** Is there anything it *doesn't* do yet? (e.g., "Invites currently expire after 72 hours.")
*   **Potential Edge Cases:** What might go wrong? (e.g., "If a user is part of another organization, they will see an error message.")
*   **Triage Steps:** Who is the engineering point-of-contact for P0 bugs on this feature for the next 48 hours?

### Make It a Rhythm

This isn't a one-off document. It's a process.

Create a dedicated `#releases` Slack channel where these updates are always posted. Pin your template to the channel description. Send them out a day *before* the release, if possible, to give teams a chance to ask questions.

A feature isn't done when the code is deployed. It’s done when your sales team can sell it, your marketing team can promote it, and your support team can troubleshoot it. Your job as a product manager is to build the bridge from your team's work to the rest of the company. These notes are the blueprint.
