You open the PRD. The little notification bell in the corner shows 27 new comments. Your stomach sinks. You spend the next hour weaving through threaded replies, clarifying the same point for the third time, and @-mentioning designers to get their take on an engineer’s feedback. You type furiously, closing comment after comment. You feel productive. You close the tab.

But nothing has actually changed. The core questions are still open. The scope is still ambiguous. The team is still waiting for a clear direction. You weren’t managing a product spec; you were moderating a chaotic forum.

A comment thread is where clarity goes to die. It turns a document of record into a maze of unresolved conversations. The default behavior is to treat every comment as a question that needs an immediate answer. This is a trap. Your job isn't to be a fast-typing customer service agent for your own document. Your job is to make decisions.

The mess happens when you let comments blur into three distinct categories.

1.  **The "Just Asking":** An engineer asks about an edge case. You answer it. The answer now lives, buried, in a resolved comment thread. It never makes it back into the body of the PRD. Six weeks later, another engineer has the same question. No one can find the answer.
2.  **The Opinion Cascade:** A stakeholder from marketing drops a comment: “Have we thought about adding video support?” Suddenly, three other people pile on with their own thoughts. You’re now refereeing a debate in the margins instead of driving alignment on the core user story.
3.  **The Hidden Blocker:** A simple-sounding question like, “Which API will this use for authentication?” is actually a landmine. The person asking knows the existing API won’t work, but they frame it as a question to be polite. You give a quick, un-researched answer, and you’ve just accidentally committed your team to three weeks of extra work.

Answering these comments directly makes you a bottleneck. You need a system for processing feedback, not just replying to it.

## Treat Your PRD Comments Like an Inbox Triage

Stop answering comments one-by-one. Instead, process them in batches with one goal: turn every comment into a concrete action. Sort each one into one of three buckets.

### Bucket 1: Clarification
This is a simple question with a known answer. The user story is confusing, or a detail is missing.

*   **Wrong Response:** Just answer the question in the thread.
*   **Right Response:** Edit the body of the PRD to make the answer explicit and obvious. Then, reply to the comment with “Good question. I’ve updated section 3.2 to make this clearer,”
