Quarterly Report Template You Can Fill In Today

Reportify AI Editorial
Staff Writer

A quarterly report template is a fixed set of sections you reuse every three months, so the work goes into the numbers instead of into deciding what to write first. If you are looking for one, you probably have a review meeting on the calendar and a folder of raw figures that nobody has turned into a clear account of the quarter. This page gives you the sections to include, a blank template you can copy into a document, a filled-in example you can read line by line, and the mistakes that make quarterly reports get skimmed and then forgotten.

The same template works for a team update, a business review, or a board packet. The audience changes, not the structure. If you need the shorter rhythm first, our guide on how to write a monthly report covers the one-month version, and the two fit together cleanly: monthly reports feed the quarterly one.

What goes in a quarterly report template

Keep six sections in this order. The order matters because a busy reader should be able to stop after the first section and still know where things stand.

  1. The short version. Open with a verdict, three numbers that matter, and the one decision you need from the reader. Three lines is enough. If the reader only reads this part, the quarter should still make sense.
  2. Goals you set. Restate each goal from the start of the quarter, with its target number and owner. Short lines, one per goal. If a goal was vague, this is where that shows up — and where you fix the wording for next time.
  3. Results against those goals. Put actual results next to the targets using the same measures you used last quarter. A percentage means very little without the baseline it came from. Where a goal was missed, say by how much and in which direction.
  4. What changed and why. Explain the two or three decisions or events that drove the results. Keep causes separate from excuses: a supplier delay is a cause, a late start is a choice.
  5. Problems and risks. List what is still open, who owns it, and by when it will be settled. A risk without an owner is a complaint, not a report item.
  6. Plan for next quarter. Give the three priorities for the next three months and what success will look like in numbers. This section turns the report from a record into a tool.

A quarterly report is not a longer monthly report. A month is short enough to list what happened; a quarter is long enough that patterns appear. Leadership reads a quarterly report to judge whether the plan worked and what should change, so lead with the verdict, not with a task log. Aim for two to four pages. Anything longer will be opened and skipped. Use tables for numbers and plain sentences for judgments, and put every comparison on the same line so the reader does not have to hunt across pages. A shorter status snapshot can sit alongside it — our status report template covers that shorter format.

Blank quarterly report template

Copy this block and replace the bracketed text. Keep the headings even if a section is thin — an empty section tells the reader something was not tracked.

QUARTERLY REPORT - [TEAM / BUSINESS UNIT] - Q[X] [YEAR]
Period: [start date] to [end date]
Prepared by: [name] | Date: [date]

1. THE SHORT VERSION
Verdict: [one sentence - did the quarter go to plan?]
Three numbers: [metric] [actual vs target] | [metric] [actual vs target] | [metric] [actual vs target]
Decision needed: [what the reader must decide, and by when]

2. GOALS WE SET
| Goal | Target | Measure | Owner |
| ---- | ------ | ------- | ----- |
| [goal 1] | [target] | [how tracked] | [name] |
| [goal 2] | [target] | [how tracked] | [name] |

3. RESULTS
| Goal | Target | Actual | Variance | Note |
| ---- | ------ | ------ | -------- | ---- |
| [goal 1] | [target] | [actual] | [+/-] | [one line] |
| [goal 2] | [target] | [actual] | [+/-] | [one line] |

4. WHAT CHANGED AND WHY
- [decision or event]: [effect on results]
- [decision or event]: [effect on results]

5. PROBLEMS AND RISKS
| Issue | Impact | Owner | Due date |
| ----- | ------ | ----- | -------- |
| [issue] | [what it blocks] | [name] | [date] |

6. NEXT QUARTER
Priority 1: [goal] - success looks like [number]
Priority 2: [goal] - success looks like [number]
Priority 3: [goal] - success looks like [number]

Real example: Q3 customer support team

This filled-in version shows the level of detail that works. Numbers are invented, but the shape is what matters.

QUARTERLY REPORT - CUSTOMER SUPPORT - Q3 2026
Period: 1 Jul to 30 Sep 2026
Prepared by: Maria Chen | Date: 6 Oct 2026

1. THE SHORT VERSION
Verdict: Response times improved, but repeat contacts rose; one hiring decision is needed.
Three numbers: First response 2.1h vs 3.0h target | CSAT 88% vs 85% target | Repeat contacts 14% vs 10% target
Decision needed: Approve two additional tier-1 seats before 20 Oct.

2. GOALS WE SET
| Goal | Target | Measure | Owner |
| ---- | ------ | ------- | ----- |
| Cut first response time | under 3h | ticket system | Maria |
| Raise CSAT | 85% | post-ticket survey | Maria |
| Reduce repeat contacts | 10% | ticket system | Dev |

3. RESULTS
| Goal | Target | Actual | Variance | Note |
| ---- | ------ | ------ | -------- | ---- |
| First response | under 3h | 2.1h | -0.9h | new routing rule |
| CSAT | 85% | 88% | +3pts | survey response 41% |
| Repeat contacts | 10% | 14% | +4pts | billing questions |

4. WHAT CHANGED AND WHY
- Routing rule changed in July: tickets reached the right queue sooner, cutting response time.
- Billing FAQ was not updated after the price change in August: repeat contacts rose.

5. PROBLEMS AND RISKS
| Issue | Impact | Owner | Due date |
| ----- | ------ | ----- | -------- |
| Tier-1 queue short at peak | response time may slip in Q4 | Maria | 20 Oct |
| Billing FAQ outdated | 4pt repeat-contact gap | Dev | 15 Oct |

6. NEXT QUARTER
Priority 1: Repeat contacts back to 10% - success looks like 10% or lower by December.
Priority 2: Hold first response under 2.5h - success looks like 2.5h or better every month.
Priority 3: Clear the two risks above - success looks like both closed before Q4 peak.

Note what the example does: each number has a target beside it, each problem has a name and a date, and the verdict appears before the detail. Nothing in the later sections contradicts the first three lines.

Common mistakes in a quarterly report template

  • Burying the verdict. If the reader has to reach page three to learn the quarter missed target, they will not trust the rest. Put the judgment first.
  • Reporting activity instead of outcomes. "Published 12 guides" is an activity. "Organic traffic rose 18%" is an outcome. Both can appear, but the outcome goes in the short version.
  • Changing the measures between quarters. A new metric every quarter makes the trend impossible to read. If a measure must change, show the old and new side by side once, then switch.
  • Leaving causes vague. "Market conditions" explains nothing. Name the decision, event, or constraint and attach it to the number it moved.
  • Listing risks without owners. An unassigned risk reads as a warning nobody intends to act on. Add a name and a date or leave it out.
  • Copying last quarter's numbers forward. Stale figures are worse than missing ones, because they look current. Check every cell before the report goes out.
  • Writing for yourself. A report is read by someone who did not live through the quarter. Cut the shorthand and the inside references.

Copy the blank template into your document now, fill in the short version first, and send that section to your manager before writing the rest — if the verdict is wrong, no amount of detail below it will save the report.
