How to Write a Status Report That Gets Read

Reportify AI Editorial
Staff Writer

Learning how to write a status report is mostly a matter of deciding what to leave out. A status report is not a diary of everything you did this week; it is a short note that tells a manager where work stands, what changed, and what needs a decision. If your reports are read only when someone asks a follow-up question, the format is probably working against you.

This page gives you the sections to include, a template you can copy, a filled-in example, and the mistakes that make status reports slow to read. The format works for a weekly note to one manager or a shorter update inside a larger project packet. If you want a ready-made layout before reading through the details, start with our status report template.

What goes in a status report

Five sections are enough. Keep them in a fixed order so the reader always knows where to look.

  1. Header. Project or team name, reporting period, author, and date. This sounds obvious, but reports get forwarded and separated from their context, and the header is the only thing that travels with them.
  2. Overall status. One line with a plain label — on track, at risk, or off track — followed by one sentence explaining why. Do not make the reader infer the label from the paragraphs below.
  3. Progress since last report. What moved, measured against the last update rather than against the original plan. One or two lines per workstream. Name the change, not just the activity.
  4. Blockers and decisions needed. Each blocker gets an owner, a due date, and the specific help required. If you need a decision, write the question exactly as you want it answered.
  5. Next steps. What will happen before the next report, with dates. This is the section that turns a status note into a commitment.

Some teams add a "done" list. Keep it to three items at most, and put completed work below the status line, never above it. The reader's first question is always "are we okay?", not "what did you do?". For the weekly version of this rhythm, our weekly status report shows the same sections at a seven-day scale.

A status report should take under two minutes to read. If it runs longer, split the detail into an attachment and keep the note itself short. Use the same headings every time: consistency is what lets a manager read three reports in a row without relearning the layout. When the work involves several teams or a defined endpoint, the structure in how to write a project status report adds the extra fields you will need.

Status report template

Copy this block and replace the bracketed parts. If a section has nothing to report, write "none" rather than deleting the heading — a missing heading looks like a forgotten section.

STATUS REPORT - [PROJECT / TEAM]
Period: [start date] to [end date]
Prepared by: [name] | Date: [date]

1. OVERALL STATUS
Status: [on track / at risk / off track]
Why: [one sentence explaining the label]

2. PROGRESS SINCE LAST REPORT
- [workstream]: [what changed, with a number] (was: [last report's number])
- [workstream]: [what changed, with a number] (was: [last report's number])

3. COMPLETED THIS PERIOD
- [item 1]
- [item 2]
- [item 3]

4. BLOCKERS AND DECISIONS NEEDED
| Issue | Help needed | Owner | Due |
| ----- | ----------- | ----- | --- |
| [issue] | [decision or action from reader] | [name] | [date] |

5. NEXT STEPS
- [action] - due [date] - owner [name]
- [action] - due [date] - owner [name]

Next report: [date]

Real example: website migration

Here is the same template filled in. The numbers are invented; the point is the level of detail and the order of the information.

STATUS REPORT - WEBSITE MIGRATION
Period: 9 Nov to 15 Nov 2026
Prepared by: Alex Rivera | Date: 16 Nov 2026

1. OVERALL STATUS
Status: At risk
Why: Content transfer is on schedule, but the staging sign-off slipped a week and the launch date is now tight.

2. PROGRESS SINCE LAST REPORT
- Content transfer: 84 of 90 pages moved (was: 61 of 90)
- Template rebuild: 12 of 12 layouts approved (was: 9 of 12)
- Staging sign-off: not started (was: planned for 10 Nov)

3. COMPLETED THIS PERIOD
- Header and footer templates approved
- Redirect map reviewed by SEO
- Analytics tags tested on staging

4. BLOCKERS AND DECISIONS NEEDED
| Issue | Help needed | Owner | Due |
| ----- | ----------- | ----- | --- |
| Staging sign-off not booked | Name a reviewer for 17 Nov | Priya | 17 Nov |
| Three redirects unresolved | Decide old-to-new mapping | Alex | 18 Nov |

5. NEXT STEPS
- Finish last six page transfers - due 19 Nov - owner Alex
- Complete staging sign-off - due 20 Nov - owner Priya
- Confirm new launch date - due 20 Nov - owner Alex

Next report: 23 Nov 2026

Notice that the status label appears before any detail, each blocker asks for a named action, and every next step carries a date and an owner. Nothing in the body contradicts the opening line.

Common mistakes when writing a status report

  • Writing a task list. A list of activities tells the reader what you were busy with, not whether the work is in danger. Lead with the status label and keep the list short.
  • Using vague labels. "Green" or "fine" means different things to different managers. Pair the label with a sentence stating what it is based on.
  • Hiding bad news low on the page. If a deadline slipped, say so in the first two lines. A reader who finds out in section four will question everything above it.
  • Reporting against the wrong baseline. Compare this week with last week, not with the original plan, unless you say clearly which one you mean. Mixing the two makes progress look worse or better than it is.
  • Raising blockers without asking for anything. "Waiting on legal" leaves the reader with no way to help. Name the decision, the person, and the date.
  • Changing the format each week. A fresh layout every time costs the reader attention and makes trends invisible. Reuse the same headings and order.
  • Forgetting the next report date. Without it, the commitments in section five have no deadline attached. Add it at the bottom of every report.

Open your last status report, move the status line to the very top, and add an owner and date to every blocker — those two edits alone will make it a report that gets read.
